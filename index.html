How Gen 3 shiny RNG manipulation works

Emerald's random number generator starts from the same seed every time the game is turned on, then advances once per frame (about 59.73 times a second). Each frame therefore always produces the same Pokémon. Whether that Pokémon is shiny depends only on its PID and on your TID and SID, so with those two numbers you can list every shiny frame ahead of time and then time your encounter to land on one of them.

  1. Find your frames: enter your TID and SID below and pick a shiny frame you like (nature and IVs are shown).
  2. Save in front of the Pokémon, for example Kyogre, Groudon, Rayquaza or a Regi, so that one press of A starts the encounter.
  3. Start the timer. An orange dot runs along the timeline and ticks during the last seconds. Soft reset (Start + Select + A + B) on the final beep.
  4. Load your save and stand ready in front of the Pokémon. In the last 5 seconds a dot runs along a timeline and ticks every second. Press A on the final beep.
  5. Calibrate: if it isn't shiny, find which frame you actually got and let the tool adjust the timer for your next try.

Tips & FAQ

Which Pokémon does this work for?
Stationary and gift encounters that use Method 1, which covers Kyogre, Groudon, Rayquaza, Regirock, Regice, Registeel, the Hoenn starters and gift Pokémon. Event-island Pokémon (Mew, Deoxys, Lugia, Ho-Oh) work the same way if you have the tickets. Wild encounters are not supported because they use a different method ("Method H") that also depends on the encounter slot. Roaming Latias/Latios are decided when the roamer is created, not when you meet it.
Can I get a shiny legendary in Pokémon Emerald with this?
Yes. Kyogre, Groudon, Rayquaza, Regirock, Regice and Registeel are all stationary Method 1 encounters, so you can pick a shiny frame here and time it with the RNG timer. The same goes for your starter (Treecko, Torchic or Mudkip) and gift Pokémon such as Beldum or the fossils.
Does it work for Ruby, Sapphire, FireRed and LeafGreen?
It's made for Pokémon Emerald, which always starts its RNG from seed 0. Ruby and Sapphire work only when the cartridge's internal clock battery has run dry: set the initial seed to 5A0 under Advanced. With a working battery, and in FireRed and LeafGreen, the starting seed changes from boot to boot, so a fixed frame list doesn't apply.
Is this cheating? Does it change my game?
No. Nothing is modified and no cheat codes are used. RNG manipulation only uses timing: you press A on the exact frame that the game's own random number generator produces a shiny. The Pokémon is fully legitimate.
Where do I get my SID?
The game never shows it. If you already used RNG manipulation for your starter, you probably wrote it down. Otherwise you can work it out from a Pokémon you caught, for example with PokeFinder's ID tools, or read it with an emulator. The SID never changes for a save file, so you only need to find it once.
Why does the timer start before the game?
The frame count starts when the game boots, which is the moment you soft reset. The first countdown lines up your reset with the timer, and the second one counts the frames from there to your target.
Which frame should I pick?
Pick one that leaves you enough time to load the save, walk to the Pokémon and get ready: usually at least 30 to 60 seconds after boot. Very long waits are fine too, the timer is just as accurate, but a mistake costs you more time.
Console or emulator?
Both work. The timer assumes the GBA's real frame rate (59.7275 fps). Emulators running at accurate speed behave the same. Turn off fast-forward and frame skipping. On real hardware, the GBA, GBA SP, DS and DS Lite all work.
The sound is late or out of sync
Bluetooth headphones and speakers add a noticeable delay. Use your device's speaker or wired headphones. A constant delay is absorbed by calibration anyway, so the important thing is that it stays the same between tries. Keep this tab in the foreground while the timer runs.
My frame numbers are off by one compared to another tool
Tools count from slightly different starting points. Here, frame 0 is the first Pokémon the game can generate after boot, the same as PokeFinder's "advances". Calibration takes care of the difference either way.
